The Transformer architecture, introduced by Vaswani et al. in the 2017 paper "Attention
Is All You Need," fundamentally changed the landscape of deep learning for natural language
processing and beyond. Unlike recurrent neural networks that process sequences step by step,
Transformers process entire sequences in parallel using a mechanism called self-attention.

Self-attention allows the model to weigh the importance of each token in a sequence relative
to every other token when computing a representation. For each position in the sequence, the
model computes three vectors: a query, a key, and a value. The attention score between two
positions is computed as the dot product of the query and key vectors, scaled by the square
root of the dimension, and passed through a softmax function to produce attention weights.
These weights are used to produce a weighted sum of the value vectors.

Multi-head attention extends this concept by performing attention multiple times in parallel
with different learned projections. Each "head" can focus on different aspects of the
relationships between tokens. The outputs of all heads are concatenated and projected back
to the model dimension, allowing the model to capture diverse types of relationships.

The Transformer encoder consists of stacked layers, each containing a multi-head self-attention
sub-layer and a position-wise feed-forward network sub-layer. Residual connections and layer
normalization are applied around each sub-layer. The decoder, used in sequence-to-sequence
tasks, adds a cross-attention layer that attends to the encoder output.

Since Transformers have no inherent notion of sequence order (unlike RNNs), positional
encodings are added to the input embeddings to inject position information. The original
paper used sinusoidal positional encodings, but learned positional embeddings are also common.

BERT (Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers), introduced by Google in 2018,
is an encoder-only Transformer trained on two tasks: masked language modeling (predicting
randomly masked tokens) and next sentence prediction. BERT introduced the paradigm of
pre-training on large corpora followed by fine-tuning on downstream tasks, achieving
state-of-the-art results across many NLP benchmarks.

GPT (Generative Pre-trained Transformer), developed by OpenAI, uses a decoder-only
architecture trained with autoregressive language modeling: predicting the next token
given all previous tokens. GPT-3 (2020) demonstrated that scaling model size and training
data leads to emergent capabilities, including few-shot learning. GPT-4 further extended
these capabilities with multimodal inputs.

T5 (Text-to-Text Transfer Transformer) by Google frames all NLP tasks as a text-to-text
problem, unifying classification, summarization, translation, and question answering under
a single training objective. This encoder-decoder architecture has proven highly versatile.

The computational cost of attention scales quadratically with sequence length, motivating
research into efficient attention variants. Sparse attention (as in Longformer and BigBird)
restricts attention to local windows and global tokens. Linear attention approximates
softmax attention in O(n) complexity. Flash Attention is a hardware-aware exact attention
algorithm that reduces memory bandwidth usage and enables longer context lengths.

Vision Transformers (ViTs) adapt the Transformer architecture to computer vision by treating
image patches as tokens. This approach matches or exceeds CNN performance on large datasets
and has driven the convergence of vision and language models in multimodal systems.
